## TLDR

- **ElevenLabs is reportedly in early talks to raise at a $33B valuation, with a close targeted for Q1 2027.** That is about 50% above September's $22B mark. Reported annualized revenue is $700M, up from $600M in September, which puts the valuation at roughly 47x revenue. ElevenLabs declined to comment ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/25/voice-ai-startup-elevenlabs-reportedly-seeking-new-funding-at-33b-valuation/), [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/elevenlabs-33bn-valuation-funding-talks-2027), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2026/elevenlabs-targets-33-billion-valuation-in-early-2027-funding-round/)).
- **Wispr Flow shipped Canto-2, its new dictation STT model (Nov 24).** Wispr claims 36% fewer errors across 14 languages and more than 15% fewer in English, compared with Canto-1. It also says the model gets hard names and terms right 69% of the time, up from 41%. All numbers are self-reported and the model isn't on a public leaderboard yet. Wispr's launch post on X reportedly got 3.3M views ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/25/wispr-flow-improves-dictation-accuracy-with-new-canto-2-speech-to-text-model/), [LaunchVideo](https://www.launchvideo.com/directory/wispr-flow-4)).
- **Murf is pitching its Finch streaming STT (beta since Oct 22) as #1 on the Voice of India benchmark across 11 languages.** It claims 50ms from end of speech to final transcript and costs about $0.12/hr. It supports 8kHz phone audio, offers zero data retention, and can be self-hosted through AWS SageMaker. Murf also offers Finch with its Falcon TTS for under 150ms combined. Caveats: it placed 4th in combined Hindi rankings, and Sarvam wasn't evaluated ([Murf Finch](https://murf.ai/finch), [FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/497518/murf-finch-streaming-speech-to-text-model.html), [Murf docs](https://murf.ai/api/docs/speech-to-text/overview)).
- **Five9 agreed to buy Level AI for about $325M in cash (Nov 5).** Level AI makes conversation intelligence software and has about $20M ARR, growing about 50% a year, for a price of roughly 16x revenue. Five9 will also sell it as a standalone product to companies on rival contact-center platforms ([CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/contact-center/five9-buys-level-ai-for-325m-to-expand-cx-ai/), [SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001288847/000119312526475778/d433495d8k.htm)).

## Where the puck is going

**Valuations are splitting in two.** Public markets value the analytics layer at about 16x revenue (Five9/Level AI). Private investors value the voice platform people build on at about 47x (ElevenLabs). Combined with Salesforce buying Cresta, contact-center incumbents are buying call analytics and QA at modest multiples. Investors are paying a premium for whoever owns the voice and agent runtime that other products are built on. If you're building call analytics alone, expect to be acquired at an incumbent's price, not funded like a platform. The tracker's "incumbents buy voice teams and bundle" theme keeps getting stronger.

**STT competition now happens on narrow slices, not overall WER.** Wispr's main number is name and term accuracy (41%→69%), not WER. Murf's is a regional benchmark of unscripted 8kHz phone calls. This continues the "finished output" theme. Average WER no longer decides a deal. What decides it is entity recall, code-switching and phone-audio robustness in the buyer's own language and vertical. For anyone shipping in India, Murf's $0.12/hr is a new price floor. Run your own entity-recall test before you trust anyone's leaderboard.

**Dictation is becoming a separate market from voice agents.** Wispr (3.3M views on its launch post), AssemblyAI's Dictation API and ChatGPT Live Dictation all treat dictation as its own product, with different metrics: formatting, self-corrections and names, not turn-taking. A full-stack agent vendor won't automatically win here.

## Signals from the field

- **Consumer complaint about full duplex:** as ChatGPT's legacy voice retirement nears (Dec 11), users on Reddit and X say GPT-Live "jumps into conversations too quickly" and cuts off their train of thought. Others say the same named voice doesn't feel the same in GPT-Live. OpenAI says nearly all voice users have already moved over ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/im-beyond-disappointed-because-chatgpt-means-a-lot-to-me-users-are-furious-that-openai-is-retiring-their-favorite-voice-modes), [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/11/openai-retires-chatgpt-older-voice-modes.html)). Fast barge-in only helps if it adapts to the user. Some users want slower, more patient turn-taking, so expose a "patience" setting.
- **Benchmark caveat:** Wispr and Murf both report their own results. Voice of India (AI4Bharat, IIT Madras, Josh Talks) is the more credible of the two because it uses unscripted, code-switched phone calls.
