# Voice AI Brief — 2026-08-11

*The infra layer stayed quiet again, but a Vermont pharmacy chain just supplied the clearest real-world evidence yet for this brief's demo-to-production-gap and recovery-loop themes: hundreds of complaints, a public pullback, and a Hacker News thread calling vertical voice AI "clueless about the industry."*

## TLDR

- **Kinney Drugs pulled back its AI phone assistant "Burt"** after hundreds of customer complaints — wrong refill information on sensitive medications, unclear opt-in consent, and no clean path to a human. The chain is reverting incoming patient calls to its old touch-tone system; the president admitted "getting privacy and security right does not mean we got the experience right." ## Where the puck is going

**Kinney Drugs is the concrete, named instance this brief's abstractions have been waiting for.** Since late June this brief has carried the stats — 60% demo-to-production failure rate, Sinch's 74% enterprise rollback figure, 21% satisfaction despite 87% adoption — as survey data. Burt is what that data looks like as an actual deployment: a healthcare-adjacent voice agent handling sensitive medication information, with (per reporting) unclear patient consent going in and no functioning human-escalation path coming out, generating "incoherent" calls about refills until the chain gave up and went back to touch-tone. It's also a direct real-world test of the "recovery loop is the real moat" thesis this brief has tracked since June 1 — Burt's failure isn't a model-quality problem (WER, latency) so much as a missing recovery/escalation design and a consent/governance gap, exactly the layer this brief has flagged (Voices for CX, EU AI Act Article 50) as under-built relative to the raw model stack. For anyone building in regulated or health-adjacent verticals, the lesson isn't "don't use AI for pharmacy calls" — Deepgram's QSR deployments and Assort Health's $1.2B healthcare business show the vertical can work — it's that forward-deployed engineering on consent UX, accuracy guardrails on sensitive fields, and a human fallback are not optional line items there; they're the actual product.

**The infra layer stays quiet for another cycle**, with nothing beyond the changelog-tier Deepgram work already reported. Worth treating this as sustained rather than newsworthy at this point — the interesting movement continues to be at the platform/model-lab layer and, this cycle, at the deployment-failure layer instead.
