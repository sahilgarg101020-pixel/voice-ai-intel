## TLDR

- **Tavus launches Griffin (Oct 1), a full-duplex video-to-video "Human Interaction Model"**: it sees, listens, speaks and reacts at the same time. In Tavus's own study, 26 of 54 people (48%) on a one-minute call thought they were talking to a real person, versus 1 of 41 on its previous stack. It reports first place out of 15 models on NVIDIA's VideoFDB full-duplex benchmark, and audio-to-video latency of about 0.43s. It is a research preview only, held back for safety work. ([Yahoo Finance/PR](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/tavus-introduces-griffin-first-face-163000466.html), [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/tavus-launches-griffin-a-real-time-video-model-that-passed-a-video-turing-test))
- **LiveKit acquires Loophole Labs (late Sept)**: all 8 engineers join. Their Substrate hypervisor targets sub-3-second agent startup for voice, video and physical agents. This is infrastructure for cold-start and scaling, not a model. ([Livetok weekly](https://livetok.substack.com/p/weekly-updates-sep-28th-2026))
- **Cartesia adds Multilingual Voices**: one cloned voice can now switch languages mid-conversation, with no separate voice per language. ([Livetok weekly](https://livetok.substack.com/p/weekly-updates-sep-28th-2026))

## Where the puck is going

**Full-duplex is moving from audio to video.** Griffin applies the same idea as TML-Interaction and GPT-Live-1: the model listens and acts while it speaks, so turn-taking stops being a separate component. Tavus's jump from 2.4% to 48% in the Turing-style test came from replacing a stack of separate models (Phoenix, Sparrow, Raven) with one model. That fits the thesis here that interactivity belongs in the architecture, not in glue code. Treat the 48% as a vendor-run study with a small sample, and the benchmark placement as unreplicated. The release gating is the notable part: "indistinguishable from a person" is becoming a safety and disclosure problem, not only a quality bar.

**Infrastructure vendors are buying scaling and startup-time expertise.** LiveKit absorbing Loophole Labs follows this week's pattern of model vendors buying up-stack (Inworld/Ultravox). Here the buyer is the orchestration layer buying down-stack, toward cold-start time and runtime isolation. The differentiator is shifting from "can you do voice" to "can you start 10,000 concurrent sessions in seconds at predictable cost." For a founder, that means the commodity layers are filling in fast and reliability at scale is where vendors are spending.

**Voice identity is becoming language-agnostic.** Cartesia's multilingual voices, alongside Qwen LiveTranslate and ElevenLabs' cross-lingual work reported earlier, make "one persona, any language" table-stakes for global agents. Code-switching mid-sentence is the remaining frontier, since many users speak mixed languages on real calls.

## Signals from the field

- **Investor take:** Stephanie Zhan (Sequoia) framed Griffin as an AI that "sees, listens, speaks and reacts simultaneously" and can coach someone through a Rubik's cube. Her example shows the pitch is perception plus conversation, not voice quality. ([X](https://x.com/stephzhan/status/2105722479453082017))
- **Builder lesson (production):** a founder's account that customers were unhappy at 3.5s latency and happy at sub-1.6s all-in, including telephony, shows where the practical bar sits. It is well above the 300ms model-slice numbers vendors quote. ([Growwstacks](https://growwstacks.com/blog/voice-agents-2026-lessons-from-production))
