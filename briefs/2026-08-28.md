## Where the puck is going

**Benchmarking is finishing its multi-month climb from raw audio quality to task outcomes.** This brief has tracked the "benchmark claims need receipts" thread since June — Coval and Pipecat naming the absence of voice-specific eval as the industry's real bottleneck, Inscribe publishing raw transcripts, and last week's academic pile-on (MTalk-Bench, Audio MultiChallenge) showing top models still fail roughly half of real multi-turn speech tests. Artificial Analysis's Speech Agent Arena is the response from the category's most-cited independent evaluator: half the score now comes from whether the agent actually completed the task (tool-calling success, task completion) rather than how natural it sounded. For a founder, this is the eval infrastructure finally catching up to how voice agents are actually sold and bought — expect vendor claims that only cite Arena Preference Elo or WER to start looking incomplete.

**The TTS price war just got a new low bar.** Murf's Falcon 2 at $0.01/minute against ElevenLabs' $0.05 isn't a marginal undercut — it's a fifth of the price at comparable latency and voice breadth, arriving two weeks after Speechify's Simba 3.2 topped the AA leaderboard while also being the cheapest top-ten model. Combined, these make price a live competitive axis for TTS in a way it wasn't when the story was purely about WER and TTFA. If synthesis cost keeps compressing this fast, the defensible ground shifts further toward orchestration, turn-taking, and context — exactly where this brief has been pointing the "recovery loop is the moat" thread since June.

**Audio-quality bundling is now happening one layer up the stack, not just at the telecom core.** Yesterday's brief flagged Mavenir shipping deepfake detection natively into carrier infrastructure. Krisp joining 8x8's partner ecosystem is the same instinct at the CX-platform layer: noise cancellation, accent conversion, and voice isolation delivered as a bundled feature inside the contact-center suite rather than a standalone API a developer integrates. Two different infra layers reaching for the same conclusion in one week is a real signal that "buy the audio-quality layer as a component" is losing ground to "get it bundled by whoever you're already paying."
