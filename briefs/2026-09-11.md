## TLDR

- **Update — OpenAI ships GPT-Live-1 in the API** (Sept 10): full-duplex voice layer at a flat $0.05/min, with reasoning and tool calls delegated to a separately billed backend (GPT-6 Astra by default). OpenAI's own numbers: +30 points on Full Duplex Bench vs. gpt-realtime-2.1, turn-taking latency down to 0.8s from 1.4s, tool-calling accuracy up to 87% from 60%. This is the API-timeline gap this brief has watched since July, now closed with a genuinely different pricing shape than gpt-realtime's split input/output metering. ([TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/openai-launches-gpt-live-1-for-full-duplex-voice-agents/), [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/openais-gpt-live-1-arrives-in-the-api-at-0-05-per-minute/))
- **Two named builders already have production numbers**: EliseAI's CTO says the new split-architecture let his team delete 23,000 lines of pipeline code (an ~80% cut); language-learning app Speak measured an ~80% drop in mid-thought interruptions in its Live Tutor lessons. ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/gpt-live-1-voice-api/))
- **ElevenLabs signs its first major-label deal**: a multi-year licensing agreement with Universal Music Group for a new AI remix/mashup platform, separate from ElevenLabs' existing Music API — voice-stack ambitions now reaching into licensed generative music, alongside Suno/Udio's rights fights. That's still well above Murf's $0.01/min Falcon 2, now six-plus weeks old with no incumbent price match. ([Deepgram](https://deepgram.com/product/text-to-speech/flux))

## Where the puck is going

**GPT-Live-1's API launch is the clearest frontier-lab endorsement yet of the "split the conversational layer from the reasoning layer" architecture** this brief has tracked bottom-up all summer — HappyRobot's coordination thesis (Aug 12), Vapi killing its own node-based Workflows builder for bounded-responsibility Squads (Aug 18), Deepgram's Flux turning TTS into a whole-conversation state machine (Aug 14). OpenAI is now selling that same split as a first-class product: a fast, always-listening voice model that decides talk/listen/backchannel/tool-call many times a second, handing anything hard to a separately priced backend. The EliseAI and Speak numbers matter more than the benchmark deltas — a real team cutting 80% of its pipeline code is the clearest evidence yet that this architecture isn't just cleaner in theory, it removes work builders were doing by hand.

**The pricing shape is worth studying, not just the headline rate.** A flat $0.05/min voice layer, reasoning billed separately, is a different bet than gpt-realtime's asymmetric $0.019-listen/$0.077-speak metering — it prices the "presence" of the model rather than its output volume, which favors long, mostly-listening calls (support, tutoring) over short transactional ones. That split lands the same week Deepgram finally answers the price question this brief has been asking since Flux TTS launched: $0.045/1K characters is a real number, but it's nowhere near Murf's $0.01/min floor, reinforcing that the cheapest tier of the market and the frontier full-duplex tier are no longer competing on the same axis.

**ElevenLabs' Universal Music deal is a smaller but consistent data point on the same "own the whole stack" instinct** tracked since Scribe became ElevenAgents' enforced default (Aug 17) and the self-hosted MCP server was killed in favor of hosted-only (Aug 22) — now extending sideways from conversational voice into licensed generative music, a different business (rights, royalties, labels) built on the same underlying synthesis stack.

## Signals from the field

- Speak co-founder/CTO Andrew Hsu: early evaluations found GPT-Live-1 gave learners "more time to think" before responding, cutting mid-pause interruptions by almost 80% versus Speak's prior turn-based system. ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/openais-gpt-live-1-arrives-in-the-api-at-0-05-per-minute/))
- EliseAI co-founder/CTO Tony Stoyanov: the new architecture let his team delete 23,000 lines of pipeline code, an 80% smaller codebase, by removing the separate components GPT-Live-1 now handles natively. ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/gpt-live-1-voice-api/))
- A Hacker News commenter with early preview access reported the model interrupting and laughing at remarks that weren't jokes, mid-sentence — "a bit rude and condescending" — and noted OpenAI had already dialed the behavior back before general release. ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48834405))
- OpenAI's Head of Realtime AI, Justin Uberti, described running a pre-launch "silent" test — processing real Voice traffic and discarding the output — specifically because synthetic load tests had missed real production behavior. ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/09/openai-gpt-live/))
