## TLDR

- **Bogotá's 24/7 citizen line (Línea 195) is now fully run by "Alex," an AI voice agent on SoundHound's OASYS (Nov 19).** It serves about 8M residents and takes up to 1,000 simultaneous calls. SoundHound says the average wait fell from 2m05s to 2s. A city procurement report for July shows a more sober number: Alex fully handled **43.5%** of about 12.7k offered calls and transferred **56.5%** to people ([GlobeNewswire via Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/425312/bogotas-linea-195-now-serves-nearly-8-million-residents-with-an-ai-voice-agent-powered-by-soundhound-ai), [Colombia Compra report](https://operaciones.colombiacompra.gov.co/sites/cce_public/files/documentos_adicionales_oc/11._pago_julio_documentacion_tecnica_1.pdf)).
- **A new arXiv study measured Pipecat, LiveKit and GPT-Live from outside, on Spanish phone negotiations (2611.19118).** Each tool call added **0.9–1.1s** in every architecture, which was more than any difference between the stacks. The frameworks' self-reported latency was lower than what listeners actually experienced ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19118)).
- **Together AI put Qwen3-TTS (1.7B, Apache 2.0) on serverless and dedicated endpoints (Nov 20–21)** at **$20 per 1M characters**. It streams over HTTP and WebSocket and claims first audio in as little as 97ms, a vendor best case. It runs next to Together's co-located STT and LLM ([Together model page](https://www.together.ai/models/qwen3-tts-12hz-1-7b), [Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/together-ai-brings-real-time-voice-to-its-open-inference-stack/)).
- **Liveops' 2026 AI-Empowered Customer report: 84% of customers have had to repeat to a person what they already told a chatbot.** 65% say there is "little point" reaching a rep who only repeats what their own AI said ([CX Today](https://cxtoday.com/in-brief/customers-now-arrive-with-ai-homework-done-liveops-finds), [Liveops](https://liveops.com/blog/liveops-2026-resolution-gap-report-why-ai-powered-cx-is-faster-than-ever-but-customer-effort-remains-high/)).
- **The FCC's revised TCPA consent-revocation rules (FCC 26-67) take effect Nov 25.** Callers can name an exclusive opt-out method, such as an automated opt-out on the call itself. Telemarketing opt-outs still revoke consent for all future calls ([Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2776/2026-11-16-fcc-revises-tcpa-consent-revocation-framework-and-seeks), [Nelson Mullins](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/alerts/fcc-download/all/fcc-adopts-overhaul-of-tcpa-consent-revocation-rules-revoke-all-narrowed-exclusive-opt-out-methods-permitted-and-fraud-alert-exemption-expanded)).
- **Minor:** Brisbane's Laronix raised a $5M pre-Series A for voice restoration, recreating a patient's voice from about 10s of audio for people who lost it to throat cancer or Parkinson's ([Capital Brief](https://www.capitalbrief.com/briefing/laronix-raises-5m-for-ai-tech-helping-people-who-have-lost-their-voice-7e24a70a-31bf-4696-a02f-0f9de0724167/)).

## Where the puck is going

**The handoff is the product.** Bogotá's real containment is about 44%, so most calls still reach a human. Liveops' 84% "repeat yourself" figure shows where that experience fails: at the transfer, not inside the AI conversation. The tracker's "recovery loop" and "demo-to-production gap" themes keep landing here. The voice agent is no longer what loses customers; losing context at escalation is. An agent that writes a structured summary of intent, entities, what it already tried and how the caller feels into the human agent's desktop *before* the transfer connects is a sellable feature, especially to public-sector and CCaaS buyers who will never reach 90% containment. Sell "the human starts at minute 3," not containment rate.

**Tool calls, not models, now dominate the latency budget.** An outside measurement puts a single tool call at about 1s in every stack, which echoes the HN "500ms+" complaints from last week and Garry Tan's retrieval-bottleneck thesis from May. The tracker theme of a ~1¢/min speech-to-speech race is moving the cost and latency fight to the backend. Speculative or pre-fetched tool calls (fired on Twilio-style eager end-of-turn events), filler speech while a call is in flight, and caching common lookups will matter more than another 50ms of TTS. Also stop trusting framework dashboards: measure latency on the caller's side of the PSTN.

**Open TTS is turning into hosted infrastructure.** Qwen3-TTS at $20/1M characters on Together, plus last week's ZONOS3-Mini open weights, means a decent multilingual TTS is now an endpoint from your inference provider, not a vendor relationship. Paid TTS has to justify its premium on expressiveness and long-call consistency, the floor the tracker set last cycle.

**Outbound compliance gets slightly easier.** FCC 26-67 lets an on-call automated opt-out be the *exclusive* revocation channel for informational calls. That favors voice agents that capture and log revocation in the conversation and write it to the CRM. The February 2024 ruling that AI voices count as "artificial voice" under the TCPA still applies.

It is a rare primary-source containment number that is not a vendor claim ([Colombia Compra](https://operaciones.colombiacompra.gov.co/sites/cce_public/files/documentos_adicionales_oc/11._pago_julio_documentacion_tecnica_1.pdf)).
