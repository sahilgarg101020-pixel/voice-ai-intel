## TLDR

- **Vapi retires its visual Workflows builder, effective today (August 18), consolidating on Squads instead.** As of August 19, existing workflows stop running; users must migrate to Assistants or Squads. Vapi's own stated reasoning: "current AI systems aren't yet capable of acting as truly autonomous agents that can maintain awareness of the current node's instructions and understand all possible next steps" — a graph/node orchestration pattern the company built, shipped, and is now walking back. ## Where the puck is going

**Vapi's Workflows retirement is worth reading as a real data point in the orchestration debate this brief has been tracking since June, not just a product cleanup.** The node-based visual builder — drag boxes, wire conditional edges, let the graph decide what happens next — was Vapi's own answer to "how do you structure a multi-step voice agent." Killing it and naming the reason publicly (nodes require an agent to hold awareness of its current state and every possible next step, and that's not reliable yet) is a sharper, more specific version of the "tool sprawl" and "coordination over model quality" themes this brief flagged from HappyRobot's six-model thesis (Aug 12) and Deepgram's Flux TTS conversation-state design (Aug 14). Squads — Vapi's alternative, where discrete assistants hand off to each other rather than one agent traversing a graph — is a bet that narrow, bounded responsibility per assistant is more reliable in production than a single agent reasoning over an entire flowchart. For a founder building multi-step voice flows today, the direct takeaway isn't "use Squads" — it's that a well-funded platform vendor just spent real engineering effort building the node-graph pattern, shipped it widely enough to need a formal retirement notice, and concluded in public that models aren't there yet. That's a more concrete signal than any abstract "orchestration is hard" take this summer, because it comes with a dead feature attached.

**Beyond that, this is genuinely thin.** Three days of checking Cartesia, Deepgram, AssemblyAI, ElevenLabs, Soniox, Speechmatics, and Gladia surfaced nothing dated inside the window that wasn't already reported or was really from two-plus weeks ago resurfacing in search indexing (PolyAI's Dialog-RSN-1 audio-native model, July 30; Boson AI's Higgs RealTime S2S launch, June 29 — both genuinely interesting and both worth a look if you haven't seen them, but neither is news this week). The pattern holds from last cycle: check changelogs directly rather than waiting for coverage, because maintenance-tier shipping keeps happening under a quiet press week.
