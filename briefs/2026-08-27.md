## Where the puck is going

**Mavenir–Sanas is a different shape of move than anything else this brief has tracked on the deepfake/trust thread.** Every prior entry — the EU AI Act's Article 50 disclosure mandate, the "voice cloning enterprise attack" thread from earlier this month, even Modulate's voice-supervision push — has treated synthetic-voice defense as something a vendor, a regulator, or an enterprise bolts on top of a call. This is the first case of it getting built into the network layer itself: a telecom operator's core infrastructure now ships with deepfake detection as a default feature of the voice path, not an integration a customer has to choose. That matters for a founder because it changes who owns the trust layer — if carriers start shipping fraud-signal detection for free at the network level, any vendor selling voice-authenticity or anti-spoofing as a standalone product needs a reason to exist beyond "the carrier doesn't do this yet."

**It's also a new node in the "voice AI infra moving up-stack from raw model to full-stack platform" thread this brief has followed since Deepgram and ElevenLabs both landed Singapore infrastructure in August.** Those were geographic footprint plays — new data-residency, new regional demand. Mavenir–Sanas is a distribution play: instead of a company buying a voice AI API, the telecom operator now resells voice AI as a feature of its own network to every business that runs calls over it. If this model spreads to other telecom infra vendors, it's a genuinely different go-to-market for voice AI than the API-and-SDK model every company in this brief's `companies` list still uses — one where the carrier, not the developer, is the customer.

**Everything else is a holding pattern.** Cartesia's GA slippage is now eleven days old against its own deadline, still the clearest evidence yet that a leaderboard win and a shipped default are different dates. The SoundHound–LivePerson vote is still one week out from its second attempt at quorum.
