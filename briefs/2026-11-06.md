## Where the puck is going

**The orchestration layer is being absorbed by model vendors.** Last month LiveKit bought Loophole Labs and Inworld bought Ultravox. Now Deepgram buys the most widely used open-source voice-agent framework. The pattern is that STT/TTS vendors, whose raw API prices keep falling, are moving up into transport, orchestration and the agent platform. Their aim is to own the default stack, not only a layer in it.

**For a founder, neutrality is the open question.** Pipecat was the vendor-neutral glue for mixing providers. Deepgram promises it stays open, and one report says the governance committee will include Deepgram's competitors. That is a promise, not a track record. If you build on Pipecat, keep your STT/TTS/transport behind your own interfaces and watch default-provider ordering in the docs, examples and hosted offerings. This extends our standing themes: bundling, and commoditizing raw voice APIs.

**Opening for independents.** As the big frameworks consolidate under model vendors, a genuinely neutral orchestration or observability layer (tracing, evals, regression tests across provider swaps) gains value. That fits the earlier "runtime supervision" and model-pinning themes.
