## Where the puck is going

**The SoundHound–LivePerson close is a clean bookend to a thread this brief has followed since early August.** OVH–Gladia and the Poland/ElevenLabs stake were privately negotiated and closed fast; SoundHound–LivePerson needed a public shareholder vote and took three adjournments purely on quorum, never opposition, then closed within 48 hours of finally passing. For a founder thinking about how independent voice-infra vendors end up owned, the pattern now has two data points that agree: negotiated deals move in weeks, public-company deals move in months even with unanimous support — plan differently depending on which side of that line an eventual acquirer sits on. The specific claim worth noting in SoundHound's own framing — 25 of the Fortune 100, one system across voice/web/mobile/SMS/social — is the same "own the whole conversation channel, not just voice" instinct this brief has tracked all summer at the infra layer (Cartesia, ElevenLabs), now playing out at the M&A layer instead.

**Speechmatics' speed claim is a smaller but instructive data point in the "benchmark claims need receipts" thread.** It's a real, specific, third-party-measured number (Artificial Analysis, not a vendor-run arena), and it's about something a builder can actually feel — a 60-minute call back in 20 seconds matters for batch/QA pipelines even if it says nothing about real-time streaming latency, which is where most of this brief's readers actually operate. Worth treating speed-factor and streaming-latency as separate claims when vendors publish either.
