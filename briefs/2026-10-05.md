## Where the puck is going

**Small, cheap decision models are being pulled out of the main LLM loop.** Pipecat's Classifiers formalize a pattern many production teams hand-roll: don't spend a full agent turn to decide "is that a voicemail?" or "is the caller done talking?" Ask a small model a narrow question and act on the answer. This lines up with this month's thread on turn-taking and false interruptions, where even Apple's Siri still cuts off longer prompts. The expected result is that turn detection and call-state handling become a pluggable, swappable layer, like STT and TTS did. If you own a proprietary turn-detector, assume the open-source framework version will be "good enough" within a quarter or two.

**TTS is splitting into premium and flash tiers at falling prices.** Microsoft's Flash tier at $15 per million characters undercuts its own flagship by about a third and sits alongside similar tiering from Cartesia, Inworld and Qwen. Table-stakes now includes 20+ languages, dozens of stock voices and a latency-optimized SKU. Differentiation has to come from somewhere else: voice design, cloning fidelity, expressiveness, or integrated pipelines. Microsoft listing on OpenRouter also matters, since it lowers switching costs to one config line and makes price comparison immediate.

**Vertical agents keep compounding quietly.** Revin's roofing rollout is typical: one deployment, a year of results, then a multi-brand expansion. Narrow workflow ownership (lead booking, dispatch, follow-up by SMS) is what produces expansion revenue, while horizontal platforms compete on price. This is a cheaper proof of demand than any benchmark.
