## Where the puck is going

**The stack is being sold as a matched STT+TTS pair from one vendor.** Microsoft now has streaming STT at about $0.54/hr and a flash TTS tier, both on one Azure bill and both already shipping on third-party routers. Add AssemblyAI's integrated Voice Agent API and LiveKit's single-key inference (both in earlier briefs) and the pattern is clear: model vendors are bundling the cascade. If you sell one layer of the pipeline, your competition is the bundled default, not another specialist. The defence is quality on a hard slice (accents, noise, domain vocabulary) that generic benchmarks like AA-WER don't capture.

**STT price per hour is heading toward a floor.** Sub-$1/hr streaming transcription from a hyperscaler resets expectations. Accuracy at 2.5% WER on a public benchmark is now the entry ticket, so STT-only vendors will have to compete on end-of-turn signal, latency to final transcript, and entity accuracy (names, numbers, addresses). The 0.13 s final-transcript figure is the number to watch, since that is what feeds turn detection.

**Voice is becoming a channel inside agent frameworks.** Photon's pitch is that users want agents in apps they already use, with voice as a SIP line next to iMessage. For a voice-first founder, that makes the multichannel handoff (start on SMS, escalate to a call, with shared memory) a plausible wedge, and a threat if the incumbents add it.
