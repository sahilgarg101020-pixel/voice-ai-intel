## Where the puck is going

**ElevenLabs is moving the moat from the model to the customer's own data.** Fine-tuning on a customer's call logs builds on its earlier feedback layer for agents. It also mirrors ThunderPhone's Zeus and Regal's Copilot, both of which tune agents on real and simulated calls. Base TTS/STT quality keeps commoditizing (AssemblyAI TTS-1, Alibaba's price cuts, possibly an open-weights STT from Salesforce). So the lasting advantage is a loop that turns a customer's own conversations into a better agent. A vertical founder who doesn't own that loop is renting it from whoever does. For the platforms, that loop is also lock-in.

**STT benchmarks are moving to messy audio and "who said it."** Scribe v3 leads on semantic WER and diarization, not just raw WER. Builders feel this: an agent that gets the meaning and the speaker right beats one that gets every word right. Expect the next round of STT launches to compete on noisy telephony audio, crosstalk and multiple speakers, where the Coval/AA leaderboards are still thin. If the Salesforce model is real, a CRM vendor shipping open-weights STT is another sign that the big buyers of voice are building the transcription layer themselves.

**Distribution keeps running through someone else's marketplace.** Quiq on Zoom follows Synthflow on Zoom, Cresta into Agentforce, and Convoso and Aircall inside their dialers. Independent voice agents win by integrating into whatever contact-center platform the buyer already uses. The selling point is passing full context to the human agent during escalation, not how well the bot talks.

## Signals from the field

- **Practitioner take:** "Half the 'latency' complaints in the wild are barge-in complaints in disguise." Callers say the agent "feels slow" or "keeps interrupting," and rarely say "latency" ([Cleanweb](https://www.cleanweb.co/2026/09/21/what-voice-agent-buyers-get-wrong-about-latency/), [Hamming](https://hamming.ai/resources/voice-ai-latency-whats-fast-whats-slow-how-to-fix-it)). This matches the Spotify "less chatter" signal: when a caller says the agent is slow, check turn-taking before buying faster models.
- **Field data:** an SF Standard report found about 1 in 3 people stayed on the line after hearing they were talking to an AI agent ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/10/29/ai-agents-calling-local-businesses/)). Disclosure costs a lot of calls, so how an agent discloses that it's AI matters to conversion.
- **Builder signal:** a DialerBee press release notes that a handoff only reaches a person if someone is staffed on that queue ([openPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4686061/dialerbee-details-how-its-arabic-ai-voice-agent-handles)). Handoff design is a staffing problem as well as a software one.
