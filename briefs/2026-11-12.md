## Where the puck is going

**The contact-center voice layer is being bought, not built.** Salesforce bought Cresta two weeks into a buying spree. SoundHound bought LivePerson's assets, Inworld bought Ultravox and Deepgram bought Daily/Pipecat. CRM and CCaaS incumbents are paying for production voice agents with proven enterprise references rather than building them. Two things follow for founders:
- **Selling to Salesforce shops gets harder.** An independent voice agent now competes with a native Agentforce option, and Salesforce will bundle it.
- **The exit market for vertical voice agents is real.** The commitment to keep supporting other telephony platforms tells you what buyers value: not having to rip out the existing phone stack. Build that way from day one.

**Voice agents are being built into dialers.** Convoso's agent only works inside its own dialer, much like Aircall (Sept 30) and Sangoma Sonella (Nov 10). Telephony vendors now ship agents as a feature. For general-purpose voice agent platforms, the defensible ground narrows to complex flows, integrations and evaluation, not "AI answers the phone."

**Consumers want voice they can control, or switch off.** Spotify's most notable change isn't the new voices. It's the option to make its AI host talk less. Together with OpenAI dropping legacy voice for GPT-Live, it points the same way as earlier field signals on turn-taking and barge-in: users reward agents that know when *not* to talk. Expressive voice is commoditizing. Restraint and pacing are where products now compete.

**Detecting synthetic callers is now a public-safety problem.** The 911 swatting cases extend the supervision and detection thread (Modulate, Pindrop BotStopper, Sierra Fleming-1). Agents are calling out at scale (Gemini Call for Me, Mitra), so caller authentication and AI disclosure are moving from enterprise fraud tools into emergency services. Public-sector buyers are an underserved market for detection.
