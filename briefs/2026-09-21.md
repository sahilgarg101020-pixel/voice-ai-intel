## Where the puck is going

**OpenAI shipping voice into Codex is a small item that matters more for who did it than what it does.** This brief flagged Cognition's Devin Voice (Sept 16) as the first instance of voice reaching a developer tool, built by a third party on top of GPT-Live within days of its API launch. OpenAI doing the same to its own coding agent three days later confirms the pattern isn't just external validation of the split-conversational-layer architecture — the lab that built it is using it internally on its own flagship dev tool. Founders building anything code-adjacent should expect "talk to your agent while it works" to become a default expectation, not a novelty, faster than the customer-support-call use case suggests.

**The Siri turn-cutoff bug is the most concrete data point in months for a thread this brief has tracked since the June 12 clinic-builder finding: false interruptions, not raw model quality, are what users actually notice.** Apple has a custom $1B/year Gemini deal, a rebuilt assistant, and months of beta iteration behind iOS 27 — and the shipped product still cuts off longer prompts. If the best-funded, most-iterated consumer voice launch to date hasn't solved this, it's a strong signal that turn-detection remains harder than model quality for every vendor at every scale, including the ones with unlimited budget.

**Speechmatics reframing Linden around specific high-consequence error classes (digits, negations, confirmations) rather than a single WER number is the STT-layer mirror of the same shift** — vendors increasingly know that aggregate accuracy hides the failures that actually break a call, and are starting to market against named failure modes instead of a leaderboard position.
