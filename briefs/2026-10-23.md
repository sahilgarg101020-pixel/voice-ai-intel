## Where the puck is going

**ElevenLabs is now competing in the speech-to-speech lane it had avoided.** Until now it was the premium TTS plus an agent wrapper over a cascade. Eleven Realtime puts it directly against GPT-Live, Grok Voice 3.0 and Gemini Live. Copying the OpenAI Realtime protocol is the sharpest part: it removes switching cost for anyone already integrated against OpenAI, which turns S2S into a drop-in price, latency and voice-quality contest rather than an integration decision. If you built on the OpenAI wire format, you now have a cheap way to test a second provider; if you sell a wrapper, expect customers to ask for it.

**The Mini/Max split formalizes a latency-vs-reasoning dial inside one vendor.** That matches the fast/slow split seen elsewhere (Tencent Gander, GPT-Live's conversational layer). The product question moves from "which model" to "which tier for which turn", and routing between tiers becomes the builder's job.

**The tradeoff critics are naming is opacity.** Coverage notes you get no transcript and no component control, so you cannot swap the STT, LLM or TTS, inspect intermediate text for compliance, or debug a bad turn. This echoes the earlier theme that recovery loops, not the model, are the production moat, and the point on silent model upgrades requiring pinning. S2S buyers in regulated verticals will need transcripts and logs from any vendor.
