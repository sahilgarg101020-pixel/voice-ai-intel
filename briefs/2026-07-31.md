## Where the puck is going

**The compliance deadline is more actionable than most builders in this space have treated it.** This brief has tracked the voice-authenticity/disclosure thread since June (Voices for CX, TikTok's AI-voice ban, the Netflix/Gene Wilder backlash) as a soft-power and brand-perception story. Article 50's actual text turns it into two concrete engineering tasks with an August 2 deadline: a runtime disclosure UI at session start, and an embedded machine-readable marker on any synthetic audio you distribute. If your voice agent serves EU users and you haven't shipped either, the grace period only covers pre-August-2 systems' marking/detection requirements through December 2 — the disclosure-interface obligation itself doesn't get that runway. Worth an audit this week, not a watchlist entry.

**A quiet infra cycle is now the norm, not the exception — fifteen straight weeks with nothing headline-grade from the core STT/TTS layer.** That's long enough to read as something other than a lull: the session-stack vendors (Cartesia, ElevenLabs, Deepgram) spent June and early July absorbing telephony, orchestration, and multi-LLM resilience rather than chasing WER points, and the model-layer leaderboard churn that used to happen weekly (Alibaba → Grok → MiniMax → Speechify) has largely moved to xAI and the frontier labs instead. If you're benchmarking STT/TTS vendors for a new build, don't wait for a headline refresh to re-test — the incremental changelog items (Deepgram's numerals support, monolingual model updates) are where the real movement is right now.
