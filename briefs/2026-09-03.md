## Where the puck is going

**The SoundHound–LivePerson vote is the first item in the consolidation-wave thread to actually run its full course.** OVH–Gladia and Poland's ElevenLabs stake were negotiated deals; this was the first that had to clear a public shareholder vote, and it took three adjournments purely on outstanding-shares quorum, never opposition. For a founder watching where independent voice-infra vendors end up, the lesson isn't about SoundHound or LivePerson specifically — it's that the public-markets path to consolidation is mechanically slower than the private one, even when every constituency (board, ISS, Glass Lewis, 97%+ of cast votes) agrees. Expect the next contested-quorum case to move just as slowly.

**Fireflies entering voice is a sharper instance of the "adjacent platform bundles in voice" pattern than Reddit's Play mode (Aug 19) was.** Reddit built TTS narration in-house to defend engagement inside its own app. Fireflies is doing something riskier: shipping outbound/inbound phone-calling agents that compete directly with Vapi, Retell, and Bland in specific verticals (recruiting screening, sales, support) — using its existing meeting-transcription distribution and Fortune 500 install base as the wedge instead of building voice-AI credibility from zero. It's also the first item this brief has flagged with a live regulatory shadow attached: the EU AI Act's Annex III classifies automated candidate screening as high-risk, and while the compliance deadline was deferred to December 2027, the Article 50 AI-disclosure requirement for automated phone systems is already live as of August 2026. Any founder building recruiting-vertical voice agents should treat that disclosure line as non-negotiable now, not in 2027.

**Everything else is holding.** No incumbent has matched or responded to Murf's $0.01/min Falcon 2 (now over two weeks old). The bundle-into-the-platform thread (Mavenir, Krisp, Speechmatics/LiveKit) hasn't added a fourth node this cycle.
