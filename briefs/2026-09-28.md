## Where the puck is going

**Google's Call for Me flips the vertical-voice-agent thesis this brief has tracked all summer (Bland, Retell, Vapi, Sela) on its head.** Every one of those companies sells businesses AI that answers inbound calls. Call for Me puts AI on the *outbound* side, at the OS layer, using a consumer's own number to talk to a business's existing (human or AI) phone line. That sets up a call-center problem nobody has had to design for yet: an IVR built to detect and route human callers now has to reckon with callers that are themselves agents, at consumer scale rather than API scale. Watch whether contact-center vendors start marketing "AI-caller-aware" routing, and whether businesses start pushing back on being called by a bot on someone else's behalf.

**The trust gap on that feature surfaced before the feature itself did.** Finding transcript-upload, human-review, and model-training strings in Google's own unreleased code — rather than in a published privacy policy — is a sharper, earlier version of the disclosure problem this brief flagged around AI voice cloning and vishing all summer: consumers are being asked to trust an agent speaking as them into the world before the company operating it has said in plain terms what happens to the resulting conversation data.

**Alibaba's Qwen-Audio-3.1 price cuts are the steepest move yet in the TTS/STT price war this brief has tracked since Murf's Falcon 2 (Aug 28) and Microsoft/Meta's STT cuts (Sept 8) — and the first time a frontier lab, not a startup, has cut this deep** (ASR −95%, Realtime −85%, TTS −70%). For a founder, the signal isn't the specific numbers so much as the trend line: raw model/API access to voice keeps getting cheaper and less differentiated, faster than most teams are pricing their own roadmaps for. The defensible layer keeps moving up-stack — orchestration, evals, recovery loops, vertical integration — exactly where this brief's dedupe history (Coval, Sinch's 74%-rollback finding, the clinic-builder turn-detection story) keeps pointing.
